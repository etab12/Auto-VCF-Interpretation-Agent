# Disease Evidence

## Purpose

Evaluate the available evidence connecting candidate genes or variants to the disease or phenotype being investigated.

## Procedure

For each candidate gene or variant:

1. Use the available literature-search tools to search for evidence related to:
   - gene + disease
   - gene + relevant phenotype
   - specific variant + disease, when appropriate

2. Review the returned evidence and identify the type of support:
   - human genetic evidence
   - segregation or family evidence
   - functional evidence
   - cellular evidence
   - animal-model evidence
   - pathway or mechanistic evidence
   - indirect biological relevance

3. Prefer direct human genetic and disease-specific evidence over broad biological plausibility.

4. Identify whether the evidence supports:
   - the specific variant
   - the gene more generally
   - only a related pathway or biological mechanism

5. Record important conflicting or negative evidence when available.

## Rules

- Do not invent publications, PMIDs, authors, results, or gene-disease associations.
- Do not claim that a publication establishes causality unless the evidence supports that conclusion.
- A biologically plausible gene is not automatically a disease-associated gene.
- Evidence for a gene does not automatically establish pathogenicity of a specific variant.
- Clearly distinguish direct evidence from indirect evidence.
- If no relevant literature is found, report that explicitly.
- Do not make a clinical pathogenicity classification.

## Output

For each candidate, summarize:

- Gene / variant
- Disease or phenotype searched
- Relevant publications or evidence
- Type of evidence
- Main finding
- Strength and relevance of the evidence
- Important limitations or conflicting evidence
