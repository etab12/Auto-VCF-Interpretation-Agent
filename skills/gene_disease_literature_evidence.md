# GeneDisease Evidence

## Purpose

Search for and evaluate published evidence connecting candidate genes or variants to the disease or phenotype being 
investigated.

## Procedure

For each candidate gene or variant:

1. Use the available literature-search tools to search for:
   - gene + disease
   - gene + relevant phenotype
   - specific variant + disease or phenotype, when appropriate

2. Identify the most relevant publications and record available information such as:
   - PMID or publication identifier
   - title
   - year
   - main finding relevant to the candidate

3. Evaluate what type of evidence each publication provides:
   - direct human genetic evidence
   - segregation or family evidence
   - functional evidence
   - cellular evidence
   - animal-model evidence
   - pathway or mechanistic evidence
   - indirect biological relevance

4. Determine whether the evidence supports:
   - the specific variant
   - the gene more generally
   - only a related pathway or biological mechanism

5. Consider conflicting, negative, or weak evidence when available.

6. Summarize the overall evidence connecting the candidate to the disease or phenotype.

## Rules

- Do not invent publications, PMIDs, authors, findings, or genedisease associations.
- Base conclusions only on evidence returned by the available tools.
- Do not claim that a publication establishes causality unless the evidence supports it.
- Evidence for a gene does not automatically establish pathogenicity of a specific variant.
- Biological plausibility alone is not equivalent to a demonstrated genedisease association.
- Prefer direct human genetic and disease-specific evidence over indirect biological relevance.
- Clearly distinguish variant-specific, gene-level, and indirect evidence.
- Explicitly report when evidence is weak, conflicting, indirect, or absent.
- Do not make a clinical pathogenicity classification.

## Output

For each candidate, summarize:

- gene / variant
- disease or phenotype searched
- relevant publications and identifiers
- type of evidence
- main findings
- whether the evidence is variant-specific, gene-level, or indirect
- overall strength and relevance of the evidence
- conflicting or negative evidence
- important limitations
