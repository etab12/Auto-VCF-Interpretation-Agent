# Gene-Disease Literature Search

## Purpose

Evaluate whether candidate genes or variants have published evidence
relevant to the phenotype being investigated.

## Procedure

For prioritized candidates:

1. Search for the gene together with the disease or phenotype.
2. Search for the specific variant when appropriate.
3. Identify relevant publications.
4. Determine what type of evidence the publication provides.

Distinguish between:

- direct human genetic evidence
- functional evidence
- animal or cellular evidence
- pathway or mechanistic evidence
- indirect biological relevance

## Rules

- Do not claim that a publication establishes causality unless the evidence supports it.
- Do not invent papers, PMIDs, authors, or findings.
- A gene being biologically plausible is not equivalent to a demonstrated gene-disease association.
- Prefer direct disease evidence over broad biological similarity.
- Explicitly state when evidence is weak, indirect, conflicting, or absent.

## Output

For each candidate summarize:

- gene/variant
- relevant publication
- type of evidence
- main finding
- relevance to the phenotype
- important limitations
